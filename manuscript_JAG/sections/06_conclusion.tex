\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study presented \textit{windowed CuSum}, a retrospective framework for reconstructing monthly rotational harvest histories from decadal Sentinel-1 C-band SAR time series (2016--June 2026) in cloud-prone tropical plantations. By pairing track-consistent 30~m spatial time series with overlapping local context windows, central decision intervals, and window-specific permutation testing, the framework renews local baselines across successive rotations without propagating segmentation errors, while two-stage spatiotemporal aggregation (TDA and STP) translates pixelwise detections into stand-level harvest events and intra-stand felling progression. Evaluated against 1,049 reference harvests across 459 Ecuadorian balsa stands, windowed CuSum achieved the highest stand-level accuracy among 14 tested detector configurations ($F_{1,\mathrm{ref}}=0.837$, $P_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.782$, $R_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.901$), raising the recovery of consecutive harvest pairs from 65.6\% under the strongest external competitor (first-post ReCuSum) to 83.9\%.

Applied across mainland Ecuador's national balsa inventory during 2017--2025, the framework identified 6,303 stand-level harvest events across 2,931 stands, with repeated harvests occurring in 59.6\% of evaluable stands. The reconstructed histories delineated active timber-turnover hotspots along the western coastal lowlands and foothills (five cantons accounting for 67.6\% of national harvests), captured the 2022 harvesting surge and subsequent market adjustment following the 2019--2020 wind-energy boom, and revealed a dominant 34-month median inter-harvest interval anchored by tropical monsoon plantations alongside longer turnover modes in seasonally drier savannah zones. Together, these findings demonstrate that decadal SAR archives can move beyond single-date disturbance alerting to reconstruct multi-rotation plantation management dynamics across persistently cloudy tropical landscapes.
